The Hundred Names

If you learned a country's 100 most common first names, what share of its people could you call by name? In Ukraine it's more than nine in ten. Worldwide it's about one in eight. Counts come from Forebears.

How it's computed

Top 100 · all is the summed count of a country's 100 most frequent forenames, divided by Forebears' own total for that country (distinct forenames × people per name).

Men / Women use each gender's own top 100. Forebears gives each name a gender percentage but not which gender it refers to, so the majority gender was assigned by name knowledge in the country's context (Andrea is male in Italy, female in Germany). Each gender's top 100 is then divided by that gender's share of the records. These columns run higher than "all" because together they cover about 200 distinct names.

Women in records is the share of the top 300 names that are female. Values far from 50% (Nigeria, Pakistan) show who appears in Forebears' sources, not the real population.

Surnames is the same calculation applied to family names. Vietnam and China are the extreme cases (Nguyễn, Wang/Li/Zhang).

Caveats

  • People with several given names (José María, Anna Maria) are counted once per name, which pushes up Spain, Italy, Portugal and Latin America.
  • Titles and non-names were removed before ranking, for example China's Nushi (Ms.) and Laoshi (teacher), Pakistan's Masmaat (Mrs.), India's Sri/Smt, and Egypt's Abo/Om (father/mother of). Hover a country to see what was removed.
  • England stands in for the UK. Forebears' UK page is empty.
  • Countries with a data-quality note are marked !. Click a country to read its note.
  • For some countries Forebears lists fewer than 250 names, so the men and women columns there may come out slightly low.
  • The World row has no gender split because Forebears' world page has no gender column.
